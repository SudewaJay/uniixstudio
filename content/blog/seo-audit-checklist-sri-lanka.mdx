Most Sri Lankan websites that "aren't ranking" aren't failing because of some mysterious algorithm. They're failing for specific, fixable reasons — a slow mobile site, missing title tags, thin content, no schema, an unclaimed Google Business Profile. The problem isn't usually that the business did something wrong; it's that nobody ever checked.

An SEO audit checks. This is a 50-point audit covering everything that affects your rankings — technical, on-page, content, local, and off-page. Work through it on your own site and you'll find the gaps holding you back. Fix them in priority order and the rankings follow. No expensive tools required for most of it — Google Search Console, Analytics, and PageSpeed Insights (all free) cover the essentials.

## How to use this checklist

Go through each section on your own website. Mark each item as pass, fail, or needs-work. At the end you'll have a clear picture of your SEO health and a fix list. Prioritise the technical foundation first (if Google can't crawl you, nothing else matters), then on-page, content, local, and off-page.

## Section 1: Technical SEO (the foundation)

If these fail, everything else is wasted. Check first.

1. **Is your site indexed?** Search `site:yourdomain.com` in Google. If your pages don't appear, Google isn't indexing you — the most critical issue possible.
2. **Is Google Search Console set up?** You can't diagnose or fix SEO without it. Free, essential.
3. **Is Google Analytics (GA4) installed and firing?** You need to measure what's happening.
4. **Is your sitemap.xml present and submitted** to Search Console?
5. **Is your robots.txt correct** and not accidentally blocking important pages?
6. **Is the site fast on mobile?** Run PageSpeed Insights. Target 85+ mobile. This is a major ranking and conversion factor.
7. **Is Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) under 2.5s** on mobile?
8. **Is Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) under 0.1?** (Content jumping as it loads hurts you.)
9. **Is the site mobile-friendly?** Google ranks the mobile version. Test on a real phone.
10. **Is HTTPS (SSL) active** across the whole site, with no mixed-content warnings?
11. **Are there any broken links or 404 errors?** Check Search Console's coverage report.
12. **Are redirects set up properly?** (Especially after any redesign — 301s, not broken chains.)
13. **Is there any duplicate content?** Multiple URLs serving the same content confuse Google.
14. **Are canonical tags set correctly** on each page?
15. **Is the URL structure clean and logical?** (e.g. /services/web-design/ not /page?id=47)
16. **Is schema markup present?** Organization, LocalBusiness, and page-specific schema help engines understand your site.
17. **Are there crawl errors** in Search Console? Investigate and fix.
18. **Is the site's crawl depth reasonable?** Important pages shouldn't be 5+ clicks from the homepage.

## Section 2: On-page SEO

Each page needs to be optimised for what it's targeting.

19. **Does every page have a unique title tag** including its target keyword?
20. **Are title tags a good length** (roughly 50–60 characters, not cut off)?
21. **Does every page have a unique meta description** that's compelling and includes the keyword?
22. **Does each page have one clear H1** matching its topic?
23. **Are H2s and H3s used logically** to structure content (and include related keywords)?
24. **Is each page targeting a specific keyword** with clear intent?
25. **Do images have descriptive alt text?** (Accessibility and image SEO.)
26. **Are images optimised** (compressed, correctly sized, modern formats)?
27. **Are URLs descriptive and keyword-relevant?**
28. **Is internal linking present?** Do pages link to related pages using descriptive anchor text?
29. **Are there any orphan pages** (pages with no internal links pointing to them)?
30. **Is the primary keyword in the first 100 words** of the page?
31. **Is there a clear call to action** on every page?

## Section 3: Content quality

Content is what actually ranks in 2026.

32. **Is the content genuinely useful** and better than competing pages?
33. **Is it in-depth enough** to fully answer the searcher's question?
34. **Is it original**, not spun, thin, or generic AI filler?
35. **Does it match search intent?** (Informational content for informational searches, etc.)
36. **Is content structured for readability** — short paragraphs, headings, lists, scannable?
37. **Are there FAQ sections** with FAQ schema (major AEO win — helps win featured snippets)?
38. **Is content kept fresh and updated?** Stale content declines over time.
39. **Is there enough content overall?** A site with 3 pages ranks for far less than one with 30 helpful pages.
40. **Does content target a range of keywords** across dedicated pages (not everything crammed into one)?
41. **Is there a blog or resource section** publishing consistently? (See our [how to rank on Google guide](/blog/how-to-rank-on-google-sri-lanka/).)

## Section 4: Local SEO (for location-based businesses)

Critical if you serve a specific area.

42. **Is your Google Business Profile claimed and fully completed?** (See our [Google My Business setup guide](/blog/google-my-business-setup-sri-lanka-2026/).)
43. **Is your NAP (name, address, phone) consistent** across your website, Google, and all directories?
44. **Do you have recent, consistent reviews?** (The biggest local ranking factor.)
45. **Is LocalBusiness schema on your site** with address and geo-coordinates?
46. **Do you have location-specific content or pages** for the areas you serve?
47. **Are you listed in relevant Sri Lankan directories** with consistent information? (See our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-sri-lanka-guide/).)

## Section 5: Off-page SEO (authority)

48. **Do you have quality backlinks** from reputable, relevant sites?
49. **Are your backlinks healthy**, not spammy or bought (which risk penalties)?
50. **How do you compare to competitors** on authority and backlinks? (Identify the gap and the opportunities.)

## Turning the audit into action

Once you've scored all 50 points, don't try to fix everything at once. Prioritise:

**Fix first (critical):**
- Anything in Section 1 that's failing — especially indexing, speed, and mobile. If Google can't crawl or your site is slow, fix this before anything else.

**Fix next (high impact, quick):**
- Title tags and meta descriptions (Section 2) — often quick wins that move rankings
- Google Business Profile (Section 4) for local businesses — high ROI, fast

**Fix over time (compounding):**
- Content depth and consistency (Section 3) — the long-term engine
- Backlinks and authority (Section 5) — builds over months

This sequencing means you get quick wins early while starting the longer-term work in parallel.

## When to bring in help

You can run this audit and fix much of it yourself. Bring in expertise for:

- Complex technical issues (crawl analysis, advanced schema, site architecture)
- Competitive and backlink analysis (needs paid tools)
- A serious, sustained content and authority strategy

## The bottom line

Most Sri Lankan websites underperform in search for reasons that are entirely fixable — they've just never been checked. This 50-point audit is the check. Run it honestly, mark the failures, prioritise the fixes, and work through them.

The businesses ranking well in Sri Lanka aren't doing anything secret. They've simply got the fundamentals right — a fast, crawlable, well-optimised site with useful content and (for local businesses) a strong Google presence. This checklist is those fundamentals. Score your site, fix the gaps, and you'll close much of the distance between where you are and page one.
