## The starting point: what was wrong

We began, as always, with an audit. The old site had several compounding problems:

- **Slow on mobile.** It scored around `[X]` on mobile Lighthouse — well below the threshold where visitors stay. Most travellers browse on phones, and many were leaving before the site even loaded.
- **Poor package presentation.** Tours were listed as basic text with little visual appeal, no clear pricing, and no compelling itineraries. Nothing inspired desire.
- **No easy way to enquire.** The only contact option was a basic form buried on a contact page. No click-to-WhatsApp — despite WhatsApp being how Sri Lankan (and diaspora) travellers actually prefer to communicate.
- **Weak SEO.** The site barely ranked for destination and tour keywords. Travellers searching for exactly what the agency offered never found it.
- **No trust signals.** Few reviews, no testimonials, generic stock imagery. Nothing reassured a first-time visitor that this was a trustworthy agency.

Individually, each was a leak. Together, they meant the website was converting a fraction of its potential — and attracting far fewer visitors than it should have.

## The strategy

Before any design, we set the strategy around one goal: **turn more of the right visitors into enquiries.**

That broke into four objectives:

1. **Attract more qualified traffic** through SEO for destination and tour keywords
2. **Inspire desire** through beautiful package presentation and photography
3. **Make enquiring effortless** with prominent WhatsApp and streamlined forms
4. **Build trust** so first-time visitors felt confident enquiring

Every subsequent decision served one of these four. This focus is what separates a rebuild that moves the numbers from a redesign that just looks nicer.

## The design decisions

### Beautiful, inspiring package pages

We rebuilt each tour as a proper page: stunning destination photography, a clear day-by-day itinerary, what's included, transparent pricing (with multi-currency display for international travellers), and a prominent enquiry action. Travel is an emotional purchase — the pages had to make people *want* to go.

### Photography front and centre

We led with imagery. Sri Lanka is a visually stunning destination, and the old site had buried that under text. The rebuild put destination photography at the heart of every page, optimised for fast loading so beauty didn't cost speed.

### Click-to-WhatsApp everywhere

We added prominent, persistent click-to-WhatsApp on every page, pre-filled so enquiring took one tap. This single change addressed the biggest conversion leak — Sri Lankan and diaspora travellers strongly prefer WhatsApp over forms. We kept a short form as an alternative but made WhatsApp the primary, frictionless path.

### Trust built in

We added a reviews section, testimonials from past travellers, trust badges, and real photos of the team and past trips. First-time visitors now had reasons to believe.

## The technical work

Design desire means nothing if the site is slow or invisible. So:

- **Speed:** we rebuilt on a fast, modern foundation, optimised every image, and got the mobile Lighthouse score from `[X]` to `[90+]`. Fast loading kept visitors who used to bounce.
- **Mobile-first:** every page was designed for a phone first, since that's where travellers browse.
- **SEO foundations:** we researched destination and tour keywords, built optimised pages targeting them, added schema markup (including for tours and reviews), submitted the sitemap, and set up Search Console and Analytics. This addressed the "nobody finds us" problem at its root.
- **Easy content management:** we made it simple for the agency to update packages, prices, and availability themselves, so the site stays current without a developer.

## What other businesses can take from this

The specifics were travel-industry, but the principles apply to almost any Sri Lankan business whose website underperforms:

1. **Audit honestly first.** Find every leak — speed, presentation, enquiry friction, SEO, trust — before rebuilding. Most sites have several.
2. **Set one clear goal** and make every decision serve it. Here it was "more enquiries."
3. **Fix speed and mobile.** Non-negotiable. A slow mobile site loses visitors before anything else matters. Our [conversion rate optimisation guide](/blog/conversion-rate-optimisation-sri-lanka/) covers this in depth.
4. **Make enquiring effortless** — for Sri Lankan audiences, that usually means prominent WhatsApp.
5. **Invest in SEO** so more of the right people find you (see our [how to rank on Google guide](/blog/how-to-rank-on-google-sri-lanka/)).
6. **Build trust** with reviews, testimonials, and real imagery.
7. **Present your offering to inspire**, not just to inform.

## The bottom line

A website that "exists" and a website that's engineered to convert are completely different assets. This travel agency didn't need more marketing spend or a bigger team — they needed their website to stop leaking the visitors and enquiries it was already capable of capturing.

The rebuild paid for itself quickly, because in a high-value industry like travel, even a modest increase in enquiries covers the cost fast — and this was far more than modest.

If your website looks fine but isn't generating the enquiries your business deserves, the problem is usually the same: leaks you can't see, on a foundation that's slower and less visible than it should be. Fix those systematically, and the results follow.
