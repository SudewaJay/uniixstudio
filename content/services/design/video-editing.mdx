## Video editing for the way Sri Lankans actually watch

Eighty percent of social video in Sri Lanka in 2026 is watched on mute, vertically, on a phone, with attention measured in seconds. Most video content shipping for Sri Lankan brands is edited for none of that — horizontal aspect ratio, no captions, a logo reveal in the opening frame, music-driven pace that assumes audio is on. The result is a video that looks fine in the editing software and dies on the feed.

We edit for the reality of mobile-vertical viewing in 2026. Vertical-first aspect ratios. Captions burned in. Hooks engineered for the 1.5-second swipe-decision. Brand reveal placed where it earns attention rather than killing it. Pace and cut tuned for platform algorithms, not film school instinct.

## What's changed in video editing since 2022

Three structural shifts redefined what good short-form edit looks like:

1. **Mute-by-default is now the rule.** Burned-in captions, visible text overlays, and visual-first storytelling are mandatory — not nice-to-have.
2. **The 1.5-second hook decides everything.** Brand-logo intros at the start of social videos kill retention. The hook leads; the brand reveal moves to the middle or end where engaged viewers will accept it.
3. **Aspect ratio fragmentation is real.** A single piece of content now lives in 4-5 aspect ratios across Reels, TikTok, Shorts, feed, Stories and email. Edit-once-export-many is the unit-economic floor.

## What separates our video editing

- **Mobile-vertical native.** We start every short-form edit in 9:16 vertical. Horizontal exports come after.
- **Hook-driven structure.** Every short-form delivery includes 3-5 hook variants — we test which opening lands hardest with the audience.
- **Caption discipline.** Captions are part of the edit, not bolted on. Typography styled to the brand, paced to the cut.
- **Repurposing built in.** One long-form video becomes 5-10 short clips by default. The unit economics of content marketing in 2026 reward repurposing more than original production.

If you're producing video for Sri Lankan social and aren't seeing it pull weight, the issue is usually not the footage — it's the edit.
