## The brief

Sri Lanka had no true peer-to-peer vehicle rental marketplace. PickMe owns ride-hail, ikman is a general classifieds — neither owns the niche. The country has thousands of vehicle owners who would gladly earn passive income, and a steady inflow of tourists, expats, wedding clients and business travellers who would gladly rent from them. The two sides had no neutral place to meet.

We were brought in to build that place — brand, platform, content engine and search foundation, as one engagement.

## The approach

A category-defining first-mover needs to look and rank like the market leader it claims to be from day one. So we built it that way: the brand carries the trust, the platform ships supply fast, and the search foundation makes the brand findable on the queries that matter.

### Brand

A friendly, trust-forward identity — the smiling-car mark, the *Rent mycar.lk* wordmark, and a yellow-on-dark palette (amber + black/white) that reads premium but approachable. Full kit: primary + dark/light lockups, brand pattern, color system, typography. The `.lk` device ties it explicitly to the Sri Lankan market.

### Platform

WordPress + Classima covering all 25 districts and 200+ cities. Listings by vehicle type (self-drive, with-driver, airport transfer, vans, bikes, cycles), direct call/WhatsApp contact, and a five-tier visibility model (Free → RentBoost → Gear 01 → Gear 02 → Turbo) that monetises without a heavy booking engine.

### Content

Sinhala-first social — engineered from engagement data, not assumption. Sinhala outperforms English 5–10× for this audience, so the social production pipeline (AI base imagery → Canva overlay → publish) is Sinhala-default across Facebook, Instagram and TikTok.

### Search

Topical clusters targeting *"rent a car in sri lanka"*. Programmatic blog engine — Python + Gemini + WordPress REST + RankMath, running on cron. Schema for AEO, content shaped for GEO. The structural play: out-niche ikman's higher domain authority with focused topical depth.

## What changed

A category that didn't have a front door now has one. Listings live across multiple districts, an automated publishing pipeline feeds the topical cluster every day, and the architecture is ready to scale with supply growth without rebuilding the foundation.

> The work continues. Supply is growing, ranking impressions on intent queries are accruing, and the next phase is layering booking infrastructure on top of a category we already own structurally.
