## The brief

St Luke's Medical Laboratory has been doing the unglamorous, essential work of diagnostics in Ja-Ela for years — partnered with 500+ doctors, running ISO-certified equipment, turning reports around in 24 hours, sending phlebotomists to homes across five neighbouring areas. The science was world-class. The website didn't say so.

Patients couldn't find a price list. Doctors couldn't share a credible link. Home-collection coverage was a guessing game. And on Google, the lab was invisible for the exact queries its patients were typing — *"blood test near me," "lipid profile Ja-Ela," "home blood collection Wattala."*

We were brought in to fix all three: design, build, and rankings.

## The approach

Healthcare design fails in two predictable ways: cold and corporate, or warm and amateur. We aimed for a third register — **clinical-grade, but human**. Trust signals are everywhere on the site, but they earn their place by being specific.

The hero doesn't say *"world-class diagnostics"* as a tagline. It shows the equipment, names it, links the spec sheet. The 4.9/5 rating is there, but next to a list of the 500+ doctors who actually refer. The 24-hour report turnaround isn't a promise on a banner — it's how the booking flow is structured.

### Information architecture, organised by how patients actually search

We mapped every page to a real query type:

- **Service pages** — one per test category (CBC, lipid profile, ECG, urine analysis, biochemistry), each optimised for the way patients phrase the search
- **Health packages** — transparent pricing, no hidden charges, organised by the symptom or concern that drives the booking
- **Home collection** — listed by suburb (Ja-Ela, Kandana, Welisara, Ragama, Wattala) so each one ranks on local intent
- **For doctors** — a partner programme page with referral resources and shared report access

### Local SEO foundation

The technical layer matters more than the words. We laid down structured data for `LocalBusiness` and `MedicalClinic`, registered the lab properly across mapping services, optimised meta and titles for local intent, and built a blog system seeded with medical insights — authored by named clinicians, the way Google's E-E-A-T criteria reward.

## What changed

The site went from a digital business card to a working acquisition channel. Bookings now come in directly from organic search. Partner doctors share a link that adds to their credibility rather than subtracting from it. And the team has a foundation they can extend — corporate health screening, doctor partner portal, online report delivery — without rebuilding from scratch.

> The work continues. Healthcare SEO is a long game, and we're still optimising service-area pages, expanding the blog library, and watching ranking movement on the high-intent queries.

This is what a project looks like when design, engineering, and SEO are treated as one discipline rather than three handoffs.
